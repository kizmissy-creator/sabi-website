# SABI Studio direction — 17 October 2026

User direction takes precedence over the old four-product Canva brief.

- Central purpose, in the founder's words: make people feel seen, comfortable and able to trust the brand. SABI Studio is made by and for neurodivergent people and people who feel different, with neurodiversity understood broadly. Belonging, lived experience, whimsy and weirdness are essential, not optional decorative themes.
- “Less in-your-face about boundaries” does not mean remove the brand story or emotional meaning. Do not make “Wear your boundaries” a mission statement or recurring homepage slogan; it can be a product joke. The previous assistant's “fashion, humour and character first” framing was an overcorrection and is superseded.
- Include I Wear My Mask All Year Round washed crop alongside Ghost Mode. The newer POD chat confirms Inkthreadable washed crop, front artwork only, no back logo. £35 delivered was a recommendation, not an approved selling price.
- The user rejected the latest type-led header and generic “odd little characters” / “A sense of humour” treatment as corporate and generic. Neither is an approved brand direction. Changing white to cream or changing fonts alone does not address the problem. Original logo asset retained; a final logo choice has not been made.
- Tell the actual founder and brand story, with a recognisable human voice and grounded details. Never invent the founder's experiences, customer testimonials, community claims or product benefits. Distinguish founder statements from earlier assistant recommendations when consulting chats.
- Preserve practical Size & Feel information and build trust through honest garment descriptions, actual imagery, clear costs and delivery information when verified, readable navigation, and accessible interactions. Do not claim sensory suitability without product evidence.
- Neurodiversity and welcome should shape the whole experience. Do not reduce this to diagnosis-based marketing, compulsory self-disclosure, a boundaries manifesto, or generic fashion copy. Keep space for customers to recognise themselves and interpret the artwork in their own way.
